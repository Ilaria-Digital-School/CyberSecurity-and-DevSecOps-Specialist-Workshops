# Reconstruct the timeline of a well-known incident

Recap: Frameworks for Incident Analysis A cybersecurity incident timeline is a chronological reconstruction of the key events from initial access to impact and recovery. It helps you understand the attack path, attacker behavior, and the effectiveness of defenses. Your goal is to transform scattered facts into a coherent narrative. Key definitions ground this task. An 'event' is a discrete occurrence, like a phishing email sent. An 'action' is what the attacker did, such as deploying ransomware. The 'timeline' sequences these into phases: preparation, delivery, exploitation, installation, command and control, and actions on objectives. This structure is known as the Cyber Kill Chain. A common mistake is confusing the order of actions based on when they were discovered, not when they occurred. Attackers often move laterally before deploying their main payload. Another error is omitting defensive actions, like a firewall block, which are crucial for evaluating response. Consider a simple example: A phish arrives at 9:00 AM. A user clicks the link at 9:05. Malware downloads at 9:06. The first outbound C2 beacon occurs at 9:10. Your timeline lists four events. The analysis identifies the 5-minute user reaction gap as a critical vulnerability.
