# Classify incidents by what they compromised

Theoretical Recap: CIA and Incident Analysis Cybersecurity's goal is to protect what is valuable: information and the systems that process it. This workshop hinges on the CIA triad, a foundational mental model you have encountered. Confidentiality ensures information is accessible only to authorized parties; Integrity ensures information remains unaltered and trustworthy; Availability ensures information and systems can be accessed when needed. Analysing an incident means identifying which of these three core properties was violated. A common mistake is to assume an attack targets only one pillar, leading to incorrect classification. For instance, a ransomware attack is often seen as purely an availability issue, but it also destroys data integrity and can expose sensitive files, affecting confidentiality. The correct mental model is to carefully dissect an incident's primary impact and its downstream consequences to determine the most compromised principle. Consider a simple numeric example. A Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack floods a web server with 1,000,000 fake requests per second. The primary effect is that legitimate users cannot access the service, directly harming Availability. While the system's integrity and the confidentiality of its data may remain intact, the primary and overwhelming compromise is to Availability. This systematic classification is your analytical tool.
