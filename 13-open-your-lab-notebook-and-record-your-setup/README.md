# Open your lab notebook and record your setup

Theoretical Recap on Documentation Practice In cybersecurity, your lab notebook is the primary, contemporaneous record of your methodology, observations, and configurations. It serves as both a learning tool and professional evidence. A mental model for this is the scientific method, applied to your virtual lab: you hypothesise, test, and document results to build repeatable knowledge. Key definitions include the lab notebook itself, a structured digital or physical log, and the setup, comprising your virtual machine's host specifications, network isolation configuration, and installed targets. Common mistakes involve poor structure, leading to lost insights, and vague entries that lack the precision needed for future reproduction or reporting. Consider this numeric example: you install a training target application. A minimal entry would be the timestamp, the exact command used for installation (e.g., 'sudo apt-get install bwapp'), the resulting version number (e.g., 'v2.0'), and any immediate configuration changes or errors observed. This granularity transforms a simple action into a verifiable record. Effective documentation is not an afterthought but the core of a rigorous investigative mindset. It underpins your ability to analyse patterns, justify findings, and communicate results, which are fundamental skills for a penetration tester or forensic investigator. This builds directly on your completed activities of installing training targets, configuring isolation, and taking snapshots.
