# Varying the scoring on an e-commerce API

THEORETICAL RECAP Risk scoring turns a vague sense of danger into a comparable number so you can prioritize corrections and tests. The OWASP Risk Rating Methodology models Risk = Likelihood x Impact. Likelihood is estimated from Threat Agent factors (skill level, motive, opportunity, size of the attacker group) and Vulnerability factors (ease of discovery, ease of exploit, awareness, intrusion detection). Impact combines Technical Impact (loss of confidentiality, integrity, availability, accountability) and Business Impact (financial damage, reputation, non-compliance, privacy violation). Each factor is scored 0-9, factor groups are averaged, and the two resulting levels (Low/Medium/High) are crossed in a matrix to produce an overall severity. A useful mental model: scoring is not an absolute truth, it is a defensible, repeatable argument. The same vulnerability scores differently depending on context — an IDOR on a public catalog endpoint is not the same risk as an IDOR on the payment endpoint. Common mistakes: scoring impact in a vacuum without asking 'what does the business actually lose?', inflating Likelihood because the flaw 'feels scary', ignoring existing compensating controls (detection, rate limiting), and mixing Technical and Business Impact into one blurry number. Mini example: a Reflected XSS on a rarely used admin page. Likelihood may be Medium (needs a logged admin), but Business Impact could be High (full account takeover of a privileged user).
