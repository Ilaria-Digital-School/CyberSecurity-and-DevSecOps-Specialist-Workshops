# Set up and document your complete training lab

Recall the key ideas before you build A training lab is a closed environment where you attack and defend machines that you own and have installed yourself. It has three building blocks: a hypervisor such as VirtualBox or VMware, an attacker machine such as Kali Linux, and one or more deliberately vulnerable targets. Isolation means that your targets can talk to your attacker machine but never to your home network or the internet. A snapshot is a saved state of a virtual machine that you can return to after a mistake. The mental model is simple: your lab is an aquarium. Everything happens inside the glass, and nothing gets out. This links directly to the legal framework you studied. You only attack what you are authorised to attack, and in this course that means your own lab. It also applies confidentiality, integrity and availability to your own work: you protect your host, you verify the files you download, and you can restore your lab at any time. The classic beginner mistakes are leaving a vulnerable target in bridged mode, skipping the checksum check on a downloaded image, and taking no snapshot before the first test. Another common mistake is writing notes "later", which in practice means never. Here is a mini example of a memory budget. On a host with 16 GB of RAM, you give 4 GB to Kali and 2 GB to one target.
