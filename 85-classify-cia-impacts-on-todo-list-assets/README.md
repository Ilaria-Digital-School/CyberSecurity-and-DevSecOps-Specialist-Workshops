# Classify CIA impacts on Todo List assets

THEORETICAL RECAP The CIA triad describes three security properties every asset can require: Confidentiality (only authorized parties can read the data), Integrity (data cannot be altered or destroyed without authorization or detection), and Availability (data and services remain accessible when legitimately needed). A useful mental model is to ask three questions for each asset: 'What happens if someone reads it who shouldn't?' (Confidentiality), 'What happens if it is modified or corrupted?' (Integrity), and 'What happens if it disappears or becomes unreachable?' (Availability). Each answer produces an impact level, typically rated Low, Medium, or High based on the harm to users or the business. Recall the vocabulary from previous chapters: an asset is something of value (data, function, component); a threat is a potential harmful event; a vulnerability is a weakness; and an actor may be legitimate while an attacker is hostile. CIA classification focuses on assets and the consequences of a compromise, not yet on how the attack happens. Common mistakes: confusing Integrity with Availability (a deleted record hurts both, but for different reasons), rating everything 'High' out of caution, and forgetting that impact depends on context. Mini example: for the user's authentication token, Confidentiality is High (leak enables account takeover), Integrity is High (tampering allows privilege abuse), and Availability is Low (a lost token only forces a re-login).
