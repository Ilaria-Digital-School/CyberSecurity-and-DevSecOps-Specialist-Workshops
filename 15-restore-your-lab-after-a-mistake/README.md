# Restore your lab after a mistake

Theoretical Recap: Understanding Lab State and Recovery In cybersecurity training, your isolated lab is a controlled environment where you install and interact with vulnerable training targets. The fundamental purpose of this lab is to provide a safe sandbox where mistakes, by design, have no consequence beyond the lab itself. This workshop builds directly upon your completed activities involving snapshots, isolation, and lab setup. The key concept here is state. Your lab's state is the complete configuration of your virtual machine, its network isolation, and all installed training targets at a specific point in time. A snapshot is a saved copy of this entire state. Think of it like a bookmark in a book; you can return to that exact page whenever you need. The most common mistake is not taking a snapshot before making a significant change, leaving you unable to revert to a known-good configuration. Consider a simple numeric example: you have Lab State A. You take Snapshot S1. You then install a new training target, moving to Lab State B. If the installation corrupts your environment, you can revert to Snapshot S1, instantly restoring State A. Without S1, you would face a complex, time-consuming manual rebuild. Understanding this mental model of states and snapshots is crucial for efficient, frustration-free learning.
