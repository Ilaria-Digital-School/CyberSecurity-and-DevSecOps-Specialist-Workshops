# Check that your lab is complete and isolated

Before you begin any practical cybersecurity work, you must verify that your training environment is both fully functional and properly segregated from your personal or professional networks. This foundational step ensures your safety and the safety of others, transforming your lab from a simple virtual machine into a secure, controlled sandbox for learning. A complete lab contains all the necessary training targets, tools, and documentation systems you installed based on previous workshops. Isolation means the lab environment has no network connectivity to external systems, preventing accidental data leaks or unintended interactions. A common mental model is to picture your lab as a sealed aquarium: everything inside can interact, but nothing leaks out or draws from the outside. A frequent mistake is assuming that because a virtual machine is created, it is automatically isolated. Virtual network adapters can be configured in various modes, like bridged or NAT, which create connections to your host network. True isolation requires specific configuration, often using a 'Host-Only' or 'Internal' network in virtualization software, or physically disconnecting the network cable in a hardware setup. For example, if you have installed two training target virtual machines, you should be able to ping one from the other internally, but any attempt to ping an external address like 8.8.8.8 (Google's DNS) should fail completely.
