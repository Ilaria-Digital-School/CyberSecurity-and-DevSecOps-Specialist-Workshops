# Identify STRIDE threats in the DFD

THEORETICAL RECAP Threat modeling connects two artifacts you have already built: the Data Flow Diagram (DFD) and the STRIDE taxonomy. A DFD represents your application as external entities (users, third parties), processes (services, APIs), data stores (databases, caches, files), and data flows connecting them, all crossed by trust boundaries. A trust boundary marks where the level of trust changes — the exact place where attacks concentrate. STRIDE gives you six threat categories: Spoofing (identity), Tampering (integrity of data/code), Repudiation (denial of an action), Information Disclosure (confidentiality), Denial of Service (availability), and Elevation of Privilege (authorization). The mental model to keep: walk your DFD element by element, and for each one ask which STRIDE categories realistically apply. A useful mapping — external entities are mostly exposed to Spoofing and Repudiation; processes to all six; data stores to Tampering, Information Disclosure, Repudiation, and DoS; data flows to Tampering, Information Disclosure, and DoS. Threats are most severe where a flow crosses a trust boundary. Common mistakes: listing generic threats without tying them to a specific DFD element; confusing Tampering (data modified) with Information Disclosure (data read); ignoring Repudiation because it feels abstract; and stopping at authentication while forgetting authorization (Elevation of Privilege). Mini example: the flow 'Browser → Login API' crosses a boundary. Spoofing = stolen credentials; Tampering = modified request body; Information Disclosure = credentials sniffed in transit; DoS = brute-force flooding.
