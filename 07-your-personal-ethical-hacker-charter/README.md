# Your personal ethical hacker charter

Recap: authorisation, scope and rules of engagement An ethical hacker uses the same techniques as an attacker. The only thing that separates the two is written authorisation from someone who has the right to give it. The scope defines what you may touch: which systems, which addresses, which techniques and during which time window. The rules of engagement define how you work: who to call if something breaks, what to do with the data you find and when to stop. Keep a simple mental model and ask yourself three questions before any action. Is this system mine, or am I authorised in writing? Is it inside the scope? Am I inside the time window, using allowed techniques? A single 'no' means you stop. The usual mistakes are believing a verbal 'go ahead' is enough, thinking that anything publicly reachable is fair game, testing a neighbouring address 'just to check' and keeping a copy of data you came across. Mini example: a client authorises you to test 3 addresses, 192.168.56.10 to 192.168.56.12, from Monday 9:00 to Friday 18:00, with no denial of service. Your scan reveals a fourth machine at 192.168.56.13. It is out of scope, so you note it in your report and touch nothing. On Friday at 18:05 you stop, even if a vulnerability is almost confirmed.
