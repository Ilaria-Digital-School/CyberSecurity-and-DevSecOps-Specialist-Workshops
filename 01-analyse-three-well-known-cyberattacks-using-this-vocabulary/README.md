# Analyse three well-known cyberattacks using this vocabulary

Core Concepts Recap You have recently learned the crucial difference between threat, vulnerability, and risk. A threat is a potential cause of an unwanted incident, like a hacker. A vulnerability is a weakness in a system, such as outdated software. Risk is the potential for loss when a threat exploits a vulnerability. These terms are the foundation of every security assessment. Confusing these terms is a common mistake. Remember, vulnerabilities are internal, threats are external, and risk is the outcome. Your mental model should be: Threat Actor + Exploited Vulnerability = Realized Risk. This sequence turns abstract terms into a concrete incident. For example, consider a hospital with weak passwords. The threat is an extortionist. The vulnerability is the password policy. The risk is patient data theft and system shutdown. A mini example illustrates this. A small business website runs on an old content management system. This is the vulnerability. A financially motivated cybercriminal seeks to deface websites for ransomware. This is the threat. The risk is the reputational damage and potential ransom payment if the site is hacked. Quantifying this, if the attack has a 30% chance and the loss is €10000, the annual risk is €3000.
