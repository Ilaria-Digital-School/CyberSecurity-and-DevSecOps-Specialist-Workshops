# Classify ten real attacks by family and by what they compromise

Recap of Core Concepts You have learned the foundational vocabulary of cybersecurity. A clear mental model is essential to avoid confusion. A threat is a potential cause of an unwanted incident, like a state-sponsored hacker group. A vulnerability is a weakness in a system that can be exploited by a threat, such as an unpatched software flaw. Risk is the potential for loss or damage when a threat exploits a vulnerability. The security of information systems is defined by three key properties, known as the CIA triad. Confidentiality ensures information is not disclosed to unauthorized individuals. Integrity ensures information is accurate and trustworthy, preventing unauthorized modification. Availability ensures information and resources are accessible to authorized users when needed. You also studied major attack families. Phishing involves deceptive communications to trick users. Malware is malicious software designed to harm or gain unauthorized access. Intrusion refers to unauthorized access to a system or network. Denial of Service attacks aim to make a resource unavailable. A common mistake is misclassifying what an attack compromises. For example, a ransomware attack is technically a type of malware. It primarily compromises confidentiality by encrypting data and withholding it, and availability by denying access to that data. Its main goal is not to alter data, so integrity is not the primary victim. Understand the Workshop Brief Your task is to apply these concepts to ten real-world cyberattacks.
